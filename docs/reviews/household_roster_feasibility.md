# Household Roster (A7) — feasibility, no model

Generated by `tools/household-roster/roster.py` from a read-only `.backup` copy of the catalog, the GEDCOM tree and the People-tab profiles. Roles and counts only — no names, dates, paths or digests. Per-file detail stays in `.scratch/roster/`.

## Rules

1. Roster = owner, spouse, the owner's children (birth order), the owner's parents and the spouse's parents; the extended roster adds the owner's siblings. A member is on the roster at a date if born on or before it; `alive` is false after the death date (alive on the day).
2. Age = whole years at the date (the birthday counts on the day; a 29 Feb birthday counts on 1 Mar in non-leap years). A year-only date is read as 1 July, a year-month date as the 15th.
3. Band = one of 0–2, 3–5, 6–12, 13–19, 20–29, 30–39, 40–49, 50–64, 65+. Candidates for (sex, band) = alive roster members of that sex whose age falls in the band; widened = age within the band ±5 years.
4. Household at a date: before the owner turns 18, the owner's childhood home (owner, owner's parents, owner's siblings under 18); afterwards the owner's own home (owner; the spouse from the marriage date, else from the eldest child's birth; children under 18).
5. Camera-operator prior = alive household adults (age ≥ 18) NOT tagged in the file, ranked: owner, spouse, then the rest oldest first. It is an ordering to propose, not a probability.
6. Truth set = files with at least one owner-confirmed People tag and a date from `user_dates` (the owner) or, failing that, `files.embedded_created_at` on a camera-native codec. Machine (detected/suspected) tags are not truth and are only counted.
7. A tag resolves to a member when it equals (case-insensitive) one profile's name, alias or People-folder name, or a tree-only member's given name; a tag that matches two members is ambiguous and excluded; a tag that matches a non-roster profile is off-roster.

## Verdict

- Yes, A7 earns a place — as a filter that turns 10–13 people into 1–3 with a written reason, not as a namer. On the owner-dated files the roster alone is unambiguous for 42% of tags (core roster) / 18% (with the owner's siblings); the camera-dated subset (95% / 76%) is inflated by clips of the spouse alone. Across every person-year 1955–now: 39% / 16%. Widening the band ±5 y (a realistic model error) drops that to 27% / 1%.
- Every tie is two or three people of the same sex and generation (children 1+2, children 3+4, the owner and his brother, the spouse and the owner's sisters, the two grandfathers, the two grandmothers) whose births are 1–3 years apart, so sex + age band can never break them and a finer age estimate will not either. The model must supply an identity-bearing attribute: People-tab hair colour broke only 3 of 16 checkable ties; relative height or order between two siblings in the same frame, glasses, face similarity to the owner's confirmed references, or — for the grandparents — which couple (one identified grandparent settles the other) and the place/event.
- The camera-operator prior names the owner first in 91% of files, but that is the rule's output, not a measurement: nothing in the catalog records who filmed, so it must show no number until calibrated. The truth set is thin (41 files, 43 tags): 22 of 63 tagged files have no trusted date, 54 of 73 owner-dated files have no confirmed tag, and camera stamps disagree with the owner's year 3 of 4 times (one DV stamp is after the tagged person's death — a transfer date), so camera-dated truth is weaker than it looks.
