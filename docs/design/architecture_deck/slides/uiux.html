UI/UX: the verbs, content analysis, the background service

Media File Operations — one engine per verb, one window: verb chip · N of M · time left · Pause / Stop · START and OUTCOME in the log

Read: Scan · Probe · Fill In Full Hashes · Validate Catalog

Repair: Repair… · Transcode · Combine · Trim · Rescue copy

Remove: Move to Trash · Delete Duplicates · Cleanup · Purge

Move: Relocate · Migrate · Retire volume

Keep: Promote · Update… · Verify Archive Copies

Every Remove and Keep verb proves the surviving copy by a full hash at the moment it acts, refuses rather than guesses, and passes the one archive choke point.

Content Analysis (cyclers in the app)

In: file bytes, frames, audio, the file’s own facts

Algorithms: SHA-256 · perceptual and sound prints · Whisper transcripts · OCR and era (8B) · date triangulator · duplicate and similar-footage matchers

Out: catalog stamps, each with its last pass and the digest it came from

Continuous, resumable, never writes media. Catalog Status reads the stamps: green reflects reality, yellow can’t fully vouch, red is lying or the check broke.

Background Service (the helper)

In: a job queue, the catalog read-only, media read-only

Supervisor: units with checkpoints · Backoff: trickle while Rick types, full at night · one resident model · watchdog kills and reloads a stuck worker

Out: observation journal → the app’s committer; background.log

Survives quit and reboot; XPC status, pause, stop. Step 1 (the 8B era job) ran 320 files on 2026-11-01: first convergence reading 51 % close enough, 88 % inside the range.

Design docs: docs/guides/media-archive.md (the verbs) · docs/design/catalog.md (jobs vs cyclers) · docs/design/background_service.md

Five verb groups cover every MFO job. The two lower cards are the same shape on purpose: inputs, algorithm, outputs. The service writes a journal, never the catalog.
